# Aesthetic Directions

Choose one direction and commit.
Do not mix three unrelated aesthetics into the same surface.

## Before you choose a direction

Ground the direction in:

- the user's actual job on the surface
- the product's domain and social context
- the existing design system or visual world, if one already exists

If the existing product already has a coherent visual identity, extend it instead of inventing a
new one.

## Functional Minimalism

Use when the surface supports repeated work, settings, productivity, or documentation.

- Type: restrained sans-serif with a strong scale and quiet body copy
- Color: mostly neutral with one functional accent
- Layout: strict grid, consistent spacing, minimal ornament
- Motion: low intensity, used for feedback and orientation only
- Avoid: decorative gradients, oversized cards, and visual noise posing as polish

Think Dieter Rams rather than lifestyle minimalism.
Everything should look deliberate, but nothing should show off.

## Swiss Or Editorial

Use when hierarchy, typography, and information rhythm should carry the experience.

- Type: strong scale contrast, disciplined alignment, assertive headlines
- Color: black, white, and one accent or an intentionally sparse palette
- Layout: grid-led, asymmetrical only when the structure still reads clearly
- Motion: restrained reveals that support pacing
- Avoid: soft dashboard card kits and vague marketing copy

Use typography and grid as the main expressive system.
This direction is strong when the content itself deserves authority.

## Japanese Minimal

Use when calm, restraint, and negative space are central to the brief.

- Type: elegant typography with generous line height and careful line length
- Color: muted naturals, tonal contrast, very little saturation
- Layout: off-center balance with deliberate empty space
- Motion: slow, gentle fades or minimal positional shifts
- Avoid: loud accents, thick shadows, and crowded section stacks

Negative space is part of the content.
Restraint is only successful when the hierarchy still reads clearly.

## Scandinavian Product UI

Use when the surface should feel warm, modern, and approachable without becoming childish.

- Type: friendly sans-serif with clear hierarchy and readable body copy
- Color: warm neutrals and soft accents
- Layout: open composition, soft corners, practical density
- Motion: subtle hover and state transitions
- Avoid: harsh contrast, acid accents, and luxury-brand theatrics

This is a strong default for approachable consumer and productivity products that should feel warm
without becoming cute.

## Brutalist Or Tactical

Use when the product should feel raw, mechanical, editorial, or system-like.

- Type: aggressive display text, monospace support copy, visible labels
- Color: monochrome or utilitarian palettes with one hard accent
- Layout: visible structure, sharp edges, intentional density
- Motion: sparse, abrupt, or purely functional
- Avoid: rounded consumer UI defaults and polished SaaS gradients

Use this only when the product can carry that amount of structural force.
It is easy to costume a page in brutality; it is harder to make the system coherent.

## Art Deco Or Geometric Luxury

Use when symmetry, ornament, and a stronger sense of ceremony fit the product.

- Type: geometric display faces, controlled contrast, and formal hierarchy
- Color: rich darks, metallic accents, or carefully staged luxury palettes
- Layout: symmetry, framing devices, and clear vertical rhythm
- Motion: elegant reveals and paced transitions
- Avoid: generic premium tropes with no relation to the actual subject matter

## Playful Or Neo-Memphis

Use when the product can sustain high energy, bold contrast, and intentional visual irregularity.

- Type: expressive hierarchy, mixed weights, and playful contrast
- Color: saturated palettes used deliberately, not as random noise
- Layout: overlaps, broken grids, and shape-led rhythm
- Motion: lively transitions with clear user benefit
- Avoid: using chaos as an excuse for poor hierarchy or weak usability

## Expressive Launch Surface

Use when a campaign, portfolio, or launch page needs a memorable signature moment.

- Type: a distinctive headline treatment with disciplined supporting copy
- Color: a tight palette with one memorable gesture
- Layout: asymmetry, overlap, or a single bold framing device
- Motion: one orchestrated reveal or transition sequence with clear purpose
- Avoid: stacking effects everywhere or hiding the actual product behind decoration

## Shared Anti-Patterns

Across all directions, avoid these defaults unless the brief explicitly asks for them:

- Inter, Arial, Roboto, or browser defaults as the automatic answer
- purple gradients on white as a generic "tech" look
- centered hero plus three equal feature cards as the default layout
- fake people, fake round numbers, and lorem ipsum copy
- card-inside-card nesting and shadow-heavy surfaces
- `transition-all`, layout animations, and motion that ignores reduced-motion
