# Frontend brief inference

Use this only when the brief is underspecified.
It is a lightweight way to pick a sensible starting point before you design.

## Design read

Write one short sentence before you design:

- what kind of surface this is
- who uses it
- what mode it lives in
- what visual language it should lean toward

Example:
"Reading this as an Operate surface for repeat internal use, leaning toward a restrained product UI
with low motion and medium information density."

## Three dials

Set these mentally before building:

- **Visual variance:** how asymmetric, expressive, or compositionally adventurous the surface
  should be
- **Motion intensity:** how much animated movement the surface can carry before it distracts
- **Visual density:** how much information and structure should appear in one viewport

Use them as soft defaults, not rigid rules.

## Quick defaults by mode

- `Persuade`: higher variance, medium motion, medium density
- `Operate`: lower variance, low-to-medium motion, medium-to-high density
- `Read`: low variance, low motion, medium density with strong typography
- `Experience`: medium-to-high variance, medium-to-high motion, density driven by the artifact

## Brief signals

- "clean", "calm", "trust-first", or "regulated": lower variance, lower motion
- "campaign", "launch", "portfolio", or "event": higher variance, medium-to-high motion
- "internal tool", "admin", or "ops": lower variance, higher density, motion used sparingly
- "editorial", "reading", or "knowledge": lower motion, typography carries more of the work

## Adjust by product reality

- Regulated, trust-first, or accessibility-heavy products usually need quieter motion and stronger
  structural clarity.
- Consumer launches, portfolios, and events can carry more visual risk.
- Repeated daily workflows should spend boldness carefully and keep key actions stable.

## Use this as a nudge, not a rule

The dials help when the brief is vague.
They should not override a clear product signal, a real design system, or an explicit visual brief.
