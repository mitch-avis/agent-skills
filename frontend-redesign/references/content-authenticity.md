# Content authenticity

Use this when the layout is fine but the page still feels fake, generic, or untrustworthy.

## Copy rules

- Prefer concrete product language over hype words such as "elevate", "seamless", "next-gen",
  or "game-changer".
- Keep controls action-oriented: "Save changes", "Create workspace", "Start trial".
- Error messages should name the failure and the next step.
- Empty states should explain what the user can do next, not just that nothing is there.

## Names and examples

- Avoid placeholder people and companies.
- Use believable, varied names when examples are needed.
- Keep example data grounded in the product domain.

## Numbers and proof

- Avoid default round numbers such as `50%`, `99.99%`, or `$100.00` unless the product genuinely
  uses them.
- Use messy but believable figures when data is illustrative.
- Replace vague proof points with a real mechanism, outcome, screenshot, state, or artifact.

## Imagery

- A strong product image, diagram, or UI state beats decorative texture.
- If the project lacks assets, label temporary placeholders clearly rather than pretending they are
  final proof.

## Strategic omissions to check

- legal or trust links the page should expose
- navigation paths out of the page or back to a prior step
- 404 and error recovery paths
- form validation details and required-field cues
- keyboard-only affordances such as skip links and focus treatment
